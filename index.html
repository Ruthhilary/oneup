The problem

In Lagos, every trip is a gamble on the fare, the car, and the wait.

A professional commuting to Victoria Island twice a day can spend ₦420,000 a month — up to 84% of a ₦500k salary. Drivers hand over 20–30% commission and absorb ₦550–600/litre fuel, so a hard 25-day month still nets only ₦272k–381k. Both sides want the same thing: a price they can predict.

How it works

Three steps, then it's just driving

01

Pick a plan

Choose Lite, Commuter or Pro. Each comes with a monthly bundle of distance credits, measured in kilometres.

02

Ride on credits

Book like any app. Fares draw from your credits at one flat rate — no cash, no surge multipliers.

03

Top up only if needed

Past your bundle, extra kilometres bill at a flat ₦135 — still under Bolt. Unused credits roll over one cycle.

Where OneUp's margin comes from

Driver cost is ₦120 × kilometres actually ridden, so the unused slice of each plan is the margin engine — light riders are the most profitable.
