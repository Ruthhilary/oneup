The problem

In Lagos, every trip is a gamble on the fare, the car, and the wait.

A professional commuting to Victoria Island twice a day can spend ₦420,000 a month — up to 84% of a ₦500k salary. Drivers hand over 20–30% commission and absorb ₦550–600/litre fuel, so a hard 25-day month still nets only ₦272k–381k. Both sides want a price they can predict.

The business case

The pricing research behind OneUp (Lagos, 2026 estimates). Riders pay less, drivers earn more, and OneUp still turns a margin — the key is keeping the driver's fuel cost low.

OneUp takes 0% commission (Bolt takes 25%), so at the same fare the OneUp driver keeps more. Priority matching plus a dense launch zone means ~25–30% more paid kilometres per shift and less fuel wasted circling, plus a guaranteed floor and tier bonuses up to ₦60k/mo.

The fuel reality (why efficient cars get priority)

Lagos petrol is ~₦1,380/litre, so a thirsty petrol car burns ~₦153/km — a driver on that can't be paid fairly at a flat fare. A hybrid runs on ordinary petrol (no special refuelling) but roughly halves fuel to ~₦70/km. So OneUp gives efficient & hybrid cars priority; CNG is cheaper still where refuelling exists.

Thirsty-petrol drivers get a higher interim rate (never underwater) and are steered to an efficient/hybrid car. A hybrid OneUp driver clears a real wage on a fare that only matches Bolt's sticker, because 0% commission and low fuel leave the money with the driver. That is the answer to "will drivers accept this in busy periods?" — yes, on an efficient car.

Where OneUp’s margin comes from

On an efficient/hybrid fleet the driver cost is ~₦143 × kilometres ridden, so the unused slice of each plan is the margin engine — light riders are the most profitable.

Every plan stays profitable even if a member uses all their kilometres (Max ≈ 8%, Pro ≈ 11% at 100% use), so breakage is upside, not survival. There's no overflow cliff: a member who uses up their plan is auto-upgraded to the tier that covers them for the cycle (never a penalty), and past Max extra km bill at Max's own ₦155/km. Blended gross at a 15/25/35/15/10 mix ≈ 24%; after insurance, inspections, payments, support, driver bonuses and acquisition (~15–18%), target net margin ≈ 8–11%. Plan-mix and real utilisation are the two numbers to validate in a pilot.

Lagos compliance (built into the cost base)

Operator licence ~₦8m + ~₦4m/yr renewal (<1,000 cars), ₦20/trip state road-fund fee, LASDRI card + driver badge per driver, comprehensive insurance and annual inspection. These are real costs — and a barrier that keeps casual competitors out.
